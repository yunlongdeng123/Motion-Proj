\begin{abstract}
Feed-forward visual geometry predicts points and cameras, but it does not say which
parts of a dynamic surface were observed by a range sensor, whether a surface blocks a
beam, or whether that block produces a detectable return. We present EAS-VGGT, a
prototype representation that adapts visual geometry into Actor-canonical continuous
free/occupied/unknown evidence while separating physical state, appearance, and rigid
trajectory. A late-fusion adapter improves development Brier score from .19186 to
.18755 over an equal-capacity no-visual model. On a separate route-selection split, a
simplex-preserving reliability discount improves Brier from .23753 to .23434 and NLL
from .95972 to .94380 on 239 candidates, while leaving the inherited surface essentially
unchanged. A controlled ordered-return experiment shows why blocking and detection
must be separate: its NLL is .86215 versus 1.05022 for a single-hazard model. We also
verify an appearance branch that gains .545 dB without updating physical geometry and
SE(3) composition with numerical errors below $7\!\times\!10^{-13}$. A frozen source
confirmation is inconclusive because its preselected views contain zero observable
Actor candidates; we report that support failure rather than score an empty set. The
results support the representation mechanisms and expose geometry/generalization as
the remaining work for a competitive system.
\end{abstract}
