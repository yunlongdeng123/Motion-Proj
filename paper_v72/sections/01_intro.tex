\section{Introduction}
Large visual geometry models can reconstruct dense point maps from a few RGB views,
yet a world simulator must answer a different question: what would a calibrated sensor
measure after dynamic objects move? A visually plausible point does not establish
free-space support, blocking probability, detectability, or a no-return outcome.
Treating RGB opacity as LiDAR opacity also couples appearance edits to physical sensing.

We study this gap without replacing the existing dynamic world model with a generic
completion network. EAS-VGGT starts from the positive components of our prior system:
Actor-canonical surface anchors, categorical return evidence, independent appearance
capacity, and rigid owner trajectories. It adds visual features as a measurement
expert, maintains continuous free/occupied/unknown evidence, and composes scene events
in ray order. Physical state and appearance share ownership and motion but have
separate trainable parameters.

The experiments reveal a useful boundary. Direct early fusion overfits, and visual
features from $\pi^3$ do not transfer in this implementation. Late fusion with VGGT
does improve held-out evidence calibration, although its surface displacement does not
improve geometry. Raw visual fusion is slightly harmful on route selection, while a
fixed convex reliability discount recovers a small gain in both proper scores. Thus
the defensible contribution is observation-aware evidence adaptation, not global dense
completion.

Our concrete contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a ray-preserving EAS data and I/O contract that keeps build measurements,
  held-out targets, Actor ownership, and split roles separate;
  \item a continuous evidence adapter and a normalized ordered-return measure that
  distinguishes blocking from detection and valid no-return;
  \item a physical/appearance ownership bridge and a shared SE(3) trajectory action;
  \item an executed comparison across VGGT, $\pi^3$, MapAnything, simple evidence
  baselines, and a neural-LiDAR capability reference, including negative results and an
  explicit insufficient-support source outcome.
\end{itemize}
