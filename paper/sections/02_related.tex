\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{From visual reconstruction to physical returns.}
3D Gaussian Splatting represents appearance efficiently~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}, and driving simulators such as
UniSim, LiDAR4D, and DyNFL optimize visual or LiDAR observations over time~\cite{yang2023unisim,zheng2024lidar4d,wu2024dynfl}.
Gaussian opacity, however, is not automatically occupancy or collision geometry. Automotive LiDAR supplies both endpoints
and measured free-space intervals~\cite{hu2020visibility}. Neural LiDAR Fields models first returns with a ray-weight
distribution~\cite{huang2023nlf}; categorical depth networks learn a metric depth mode~\cite{reading2021caddn}; QueryOcc
directly samples positive and negative continuous queries~\cite{lilja2026queryocc}. We retain their relevant boundary:
native rays supervise the physical representation, while an Actor-local surface and its termination distribution remain
different typed objects.

\paragraph{Completion and evidential occupancy.}
Object-centric occupancy completion canonicalizes long LiDAR tracklets before implicit decoding~\cite{zheng2024objectcentric},
while SelfOcc and SparseOcc++ learn continuous geometry fields~\cite{huang2024selfocc,tang2026sparseoccplusplus}.
PoinTr translates point sets and SnowflakeNet grows child points from parent seeds~\cite{yu2021pointr,xiang2021snowflakenet}.
Gau-Occ initializes Gaussians from diffusion-completed LiDAR, but its multimodal occupancy head may still update center,
scale, and rotation~\cite{lv2026gauocc}. We borrow target/set completion, not unconstrained image-conditioned geometry:
dynamic sweeps are Actor-motion compensated, observed anchors are immutable, and geometry/ray losses remain active throughout.

ALSO and evidential occupancy derive FREE/OCCUPIED supervision from the sensor origin~\cite{boulch2023also,kalble2024accurate,kalble2025evocc}.
GaussianFormer-2 and GaussRender connect Gaussian representations to occupancy and projective supervision
~\cite{huang2025gaussianformer2,chambon2025gaussrender}; Vol3DGS analytically integrates Gaussians into transmittance
~\cite{talegaonkar2025vol3dgs}. Our negative optical ablations expose a complementary issue: additive thickness changes
with primitive density. We instead use producer evidence as categorical surface-return authority and state exactly what its
normalized CDF can and cannot guarantee.

\paragraph{Dynamic factorization and appearance ownership.}
Multi-frame completion labels can contain moving-object traces~\cite{xia2023scpnet}. DualAD separates static and dynamic
streams with ego/object-motion compensation~\cite{doll2024dualad}; DynamicVGGT predicts Gaussian velocity under scene-flow
supervision~\cite{he2026dynamicvggt}; DeGO separates rigid offsets and non-rigid deformation~\cite{gao2026dego}; and
SelfOccFlow maintains separate static/dynamic signed-distance fields~\cite{timoneda2026selfoccflow}. These works motivate
our refusal to hide motion inside a canonical shape head. We use annotated rigid pose only as a read-only transform; non-rigid
evolution remains unsupported without its own target. Likewise, Feature 3DGS and Neural Shell Texture Splatting show how
visual attributes can live on geometry~\cite{zhou2024feature3dgs,zhang2025shelltexture}. Our stricter ownership contract gives
image gradients a sibling visual state and no path to physical center or scale.

\paragraph{Cross-sensor evaluation.}
nuScenes and Argoverse~2 provide calibrated but materially different multi-sensor trajectories
~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,wilson2021argoverse2}. LiDAR domain shift includes sampling pattern as well as scene statistics;
source-only density consistency and accumulated sequence geometry can improve transfer
~\cite{kim2023dglss,sanchez2023labelprop}. They do not certify Actor-level physical transfer. We therefore freeze one AV2
cohort before reading any learned output and prohibit target adaptation, calibration, or failed-log deletion.
