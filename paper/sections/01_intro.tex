\section{Introduction}

Reconstructed driving worlds are increasingly used for perception analysis, counterfactual replay, and planning evaluation. Their geometry, however, is rarely a literal collision model. A rendered Gaussian may explain image appearance while occupying free space, duplicating an Actor shell, or flickering across time. Conversely, a perfectly valid Actor may be difficult because it cuts in, brakes hard, or crosses the Ego path. A useful world compiler must distinguish these cases: \emph{world validity is not world difficulty}.

This distinction matters because a generic confidence filter has an easy but scientifically undesirable failure mode. It can improve aggregate collision proxies by suppressing uncertain or nearby Actors. Such a world looks safer because legitimate hazards disappeared, not because its physical state became more faithful. We instead ask whether evidence-inconsistent geometry can be repaired while identity, lifecycle, trajectory, size, time-to-collision, and hazard events remain fixed.

We propose \method, a physical compilation layer between a reconstructed scene and downstream task queries. It represents a world as
\begin{equation}
\world_t=(\appearance_t,\surface_t,\actors_t,\provenance_t,\validity_t),
\end{equation}
where appearance Gaussians and physical collision surfaces have separate semantics. The compiler uses multi-frame Actor alignment, ray free/occupied evidence, temporal support, and source provenance to select one of four local actions. Unsupported regions become \textsc{unknown}, never free by default, while Actors remain in the scene.

Our second component factorizes validity from hazard with disjoint inputs and paired counterfactual diagnostics. Changing a clean Actor from ordinary driving to a legal near-miss should not change its repair score; injecting a duplicate shell or temporal pop should not change its hazard score. A selective interface repairs an Actor only when nuScenes-calibrated runtime evidence supports it and otherwise returns the clean query. Our third component links physical state quality to downstream reliability. An Actor residual distribution is projected onto a candidate trajectory boundary, yielding a continuous cost whose conditional density supports arbitrary budgets and multiple horizons.

We then replace deployment-time selection by supervision-native Actor-local completion. Motion-corrected held-out LiDAR
defines the target before training; observed anchors are immutable, and every generated child remains in the deployed
surface. Target-set, local-plane, scale, frame-coverage, literal first-return, and free-before-hit losses remain active
together. This produces a source-domain geometry Pareto rather than a post-hoc filter. Because physical surface support
and sensor termination are different objects, a second learned interface maps producer-side evidence to continuous
FREE/OCCUPIED/UNKNOWN authority and composes it as a categorical return measure.

The failure sequence yields a safety result of independent interest. Additive Gaussian optical thickness depends on
primitive density, while normalized return mixtures make attenuation non-monotone: suppressing a component can move
probability toward, rather than away from, the early-return boundary. We derive the exact finite-change sign and verify it
on 99,208 frozen rays. Appearance and rigid pose are separate typed authorities, so neither can silently repair geometry.
External generalization is evaluated only once after all AV2 logs are complete.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a hazard-preserving compiler and supervision-native Actor surface whose geometry is defined by motion-corrected GT
    targets, literal rays, and free-space intervals rather than UNKNOWN deletion;
    \item producer-evidential FREE/OCCUPIED/UNKNOWN authority composed as a categorical surface-return measure, separating
    learned 3D evidence from density-dependent optical opacity;
    \item typed physical, pose, static-world, and appearance ownership with inverse-query SE(3) equivariance and no image
    gradient path to collision geometry;
    \item an exact finite attenuation boundary for normalized return mixtures, plus a frozen nuScenes-to-AV2 protocol that
    withholds cross-domain claims until its complete aggregate is available.
\end{itemize}
