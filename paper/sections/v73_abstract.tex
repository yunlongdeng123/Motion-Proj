\begin{abstract}
We study reconstruction of a reusable, metric surface for each rigid actor from sparse multi-time observations. A single canonical surface must explain measured points and remain consistent when queried from different poses. This task exposes a gap between geometric coverage and first-surface correctness: a surface can lie close to a measured endpoint while a different part intersects its ray too early. We formulate queryable canonical surfaces and develop witness-constrained charts, separating surface placement, support domain, and observation responsibility. An oracle ladder and exact triangle forensics on 75 development actors show that well-shaped, observation-supported patches can still create incorrect first intersections. Across seven saved representations, none of the early-hit triangles exceeds the stated sliver threshold. The completed joint r7 pathway improves correct first intersections by 10.54 percentage points over its LiDAR control, but preservation of free-space consistency is not established. We derive a sparse first-intersection adjoint and an incidence-normalized orthogonal update that protects fixed first-hit witnesses during completion. The new operator passes numerical checks; learned support domains and end-to-end orthogonal training remain unvalidated. Completed experiments, checked prototypes, and proposed components are reported separately. The manuscript advances a bounded reconstruction task and testable method, without claiming a finished simulator or established overall superiority.
\end{abstract}
