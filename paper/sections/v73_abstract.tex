\begin{abstract}
We study reconstruction of a reusable, metric surface for each rigid actor from sparse multi-time observations. A single canonical surface must explain measured points and remain consistent when queried from different poses. This task exposes a gap between geometric coverage and first-surface correctness: a surface can lie close to a measured endpoint while a different part intersects its ray too early. We formulate queryable canonical surfaces and develop witness-constrained charts, separating surface placement, support domain, and observation responsibility. An oracle ladder and exact triangle forensics on 75 development actors show that well-shaped, observation-supported patches can still create incorrect first intersections. Across seven saved representations, none of the early-hit triangles exceeds the stated sliver threshold. The completed joint r7 pathway improves correct first intersections by 10.54 percentage points over its LiDAR control on five development logs. However, fixed confirmation on 20 independent AV2 logs reverses this gain: hits fall 2.94 points and free intrusion rises 0.326 m, with adverse log intervals excluding zero. The generalizable joint geometry-and-physics hypothesis fails this confirmation. We derive a sparse first-intersection adjoint and an incidence-normalized orthogonal update that protects fixed first-hit witnesses during completion. The new operator passes numerical checks; learned support domains and end-to-end orthogonal training remain unvalidated. Completed experiments, checked prototypes, and proposed components are reported separately. The manuscript advances a bounded reconstruction task and testable method, without claiming a finished simulator or established overall superiority.
\end{abstract}
